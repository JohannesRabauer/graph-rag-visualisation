The graph

Communities

A community is a cluster of entities that are linked to each other. The shaded regions and the legend chips at the bottom left show them; Global and Drift Search read their summaries.

Overview

Click a legend chip to focus its community. The legend keeps to one line with the largest communities first; the "All communities" button at its end lists every community by size in the side panel, and picking one there opens it.

The idea

GraphRAG clusters the entity graph (the original work uses the Leiden algorithm) and has an LLM write a report for each cluster. Those reports let global questions be answered without reading every chunk.

In this demo

DetectCommunities uses Neo4j Graph Data Science Leiden clustering over the corpus graph (relationships weighted by how many passages state them, fixed seed so a rebuild groups the same way), so densely linked entities share a community even when a bridge relationship links them to another cluster. Without Neo4j (in-memory or offline) it falls back to connected components. Only a group of at least three entities becomes a community (DetectCommunities.MIN_COMMUNITY_SIZE): an entity without relationships, or a pair linked only to each other, stays a plain node in the graph with no hull and no legend chip, gets no summary, and is not used by Global or Drift Search. Local Search still finds it. Each community gets an id such as community-1 (numbered without gaps over the communities that are kept), a short title and a summary. With an API key LlmPort writes both from the community's content: its members with their descriptions (up to 25) and the relationships between them with their descriptions (up to 30, highest weight first), one call per community. The title (at most six words) names the community in the legend, on its hull and in the detail panel, which also shows the full summary. Without an API key the title is the first two entity names joined with "&", and the summary is the template "This community centers on" followed by up to four entity names. Communities from older corpora have no title and are labelled with the start of their summary. The result is persisted and announced as community-detected events.

Readers of communities: Global Search and Drift Search.

Simplifications

  • One flat level of Leiden communities; there is no hierarchy of levels. In-memory and offline runs use connected components, where one bridge relationship merges two clusters.
  • Detection always runs; the formation view is visual only.
  • Groups smaller than three entities are dropped rather than merged into a neighbouring community. A corpus whose groups are all that small has no communities, so Global and Drift Search have nothing to read.
